# FoodRescue — Project Foundation

## 1.1 Project Overview

The Smart Food Surplus Management & Redistribution System, also known as FoodRescue, is a centralized software platform designed to manage the redistribution of surplus food from donors to eligible recipients.

The system connects four primary user roles: donors, recipients, volunteers, and administrators. Donors can register surplus food and provide details such as quantity, preparation time, expiry time, and pickup location. Recipients can discover suitable available food and submit requests, while volunteers can coordinate pickups and deliveries. Administrators oversee users, listings, requests, and system activity.

The system manages the surplus-food lifecycle through a structured workflow involving listing, validation, prioritization, recipient matching, requesting, allocation, collection, delivery, and impact tracking.

FoodRescue is designed primarily as a Software Engineering system rather than an Artificial Intelligence or Machine Learning project. Its "smart" functionality is based on transparent rule-based prioritization and matching logic.


## 1.2 Background & Motivation

Food surplus can arise in a variety of settings, including households, restaurants, cafeterias, hostels, events, and other food-related establishments. Although some of this surplus may still be suitable for consumption, effective redistribution can be difficult when information about available food, its quantity, location, and expiry is not organized in a centralized system.

The redistribution process also involves multiple participants with different responsibilities. Donors need a way to report available surplus, recipients need to identify suitable food and submit requests, volunteers may be required to coordinate collection and delivery, and administrators need to supervise the overall process.

These requirements motivate the development of FoodRescue as a centralized software system that organizes the surplus-food redistribution lifecycle. The system is intended to make the process more structured, time-sensitive, and trackable by bringing listing, prioritization, recipient matching, requests, allocation, pickup, delivery, notifications, and impact tracking into one platform.


## 1.3 Problem Statement

Edible surplus food generated by households, restaurants, cafeterias, hostels, events, and other establishments is often not efficiently redistributed because there is no centralized system for registering surplus food, tracking its quantity and expiry, identifying suitable recipients, coordinating collection and delivery, and recording successful redistribution.

This project proposes a software-based Smart Food Surplus Management and Redistribution System that connects donors, recipients, volunteers, and administrators through a structured digital workflow to improve the management of surplus food and reduce avoidable food waste.


## 1.4 Objectives

The main objective of FoodRescue is to develop a centralized software system that enables surplus food to be registered, prioritized, matched with suitable recipients, collected, and tracked through a structured workflow in order to reduce avoidable food waste.

The specific objectives of the system are:

1. To provide a centralized platform for registering and managing surplus food listings.

2. To allow donors to record important food information such as quantity, preparation time, location, and expiry or use-by time.

3. To enable eligible recipients to search for, view, and request suitable surplus food.

4. To provide rule-based prioritization of food listings based on factors such as expiry urgency, quantity, distance, recipient suitability, and listing age.

5. To support the matching and allocation of surplus food with suitable recipients.

6. To coordinate pickup and delivery through volunteer assignments and status tracking.

7. To manage food expiry and prevent unavailable or expired listings from being requested.

8. To provide role-based access for donors, recipients, volunteers, and administrators.

9. To generate notifications for important events such as requests, approvals, assignments, cancellations, and approaching expiry.

10. To maintain records of successful and unsuccessful redistribution activities and provide basic impact statistics.

11. To provide a modular, maintainable, reliable, and secure software system that can be extended in the future.


## 1.5 Project Scope

The scope of FoodRescue covers the digital management of surplus food from the point at which it is listed by a donor to the point at which it is successfully collected, delivered, and recorded as redistributed.

### 1.5.1 In-Scope Features

The system will include:

1. User registration and login for donors, recipients, volunteers, and administrators.

2. Role-based access control so that users can access only the functions permitted for their role.

3. Donor management of surplus-food listings, including food name, category, quantity, preparation time, listing time, expiry or use-by time, dietary type, pickup location, and other relevant information.

4. Browsing, searching, and filtering of available surplus food by eligible recipients.

5. Submission, approval, rejection, cancellation, and tracking of food requests.

6. Rule-based prioritization of food listings using factors such as expiry urgency, quantity, distance, recipient suitability, and listing age.

7. Rule-based matching of suitable recipients with available surplus food.

8. Allocation of approved food requests.

9. Volunteer assignment and management of pickup and delivery activities.

10. Tracking of food listing, request, allocation, pickup, and delivery statuses.

11. Expiry handling, including marking expired listings as unavailable and preventing new requests for them.

12. In-app notifications for important system events.

13. Role-specific dashboards and basic impact statistics.

14. Donation and redistribution history.

15. Administrative monitoring and management of users, listings, requests, and system activity.

### 1.5.2 Out-of-Scope Features

The initial version of the system will not include:

1. Mobile applications.

2. GPS-based live tracking.

3. SMS or WhatsApp notification services.

4. Advanced route optimization.

5. Machine Learning or AI-based prediction as a core system component.

6. Image-based food recognition.

7. IoT-based inventory monitoring.

8. Dynamic pricing or commercial food-ordering functionality.

9. Multi-city production deployment.

These features may be considered as future enhancements after the core system has been implemented and tested.


## 1.6 Target Users / Actors

FoodRescue is a multi-user system with four primary categories of actors. Each actor has a defined role and interacts with the system according to their responsibilities.

### 1.6.1 Donor

A donor is a person or organization that provides surplus food through the platform.

Examples include:

- Restaurants
- Cafeterias
- College canteens
- Hostels
- Event organizers
- Grocery or food establishments
- Households

Donor responsibilities and functions include:

- Registering and logging into the system
- Creating surplus-food listings
- Entering food details and available quantity
- Providing preparation, listing, expiry, and pickup information
- Viewing active listings
- Viewing received requests
- Approving or rejecting requests
- Confirming food handover
- Viewing donation history
- Viewing basic impact statistics

### 1.6.2 Recipient

A recipient is an eligible individual or organization that can receive surplus food.

Examples include:

- NGOs
- Shelters
- Food banks
- Community organizations
- Other approved recipient groups

Recipient responsibilities and functions include:

- Registering and maintaining profile information
- Viewing available surplus food
- Searching and filtering food listings
- Viewing food details
- Submitting food requests
- Tracking request status
- Viewing approved allocations
- Confirming receipt
- Viewing request and receipt history

Recipient eligibility may be verified and approved by the administrator.

### 1.6.3 Volunteer / Delivery Coordinator

A volunteer assists with the physical collection and delivery of allocated surplus food.

Volunteer responsibilities and functions include:

- Registering and logging into the system
- Viewing assigned pickup and delivery tasks
- Accepting or rejecting assignments
- Viewing pickup and delivery details
- Updating pickup and delivery statuses
- Confirming food collection
- Confirming delivery
- Viewing completed assignments and history

### 1.6.4 Administrator

The administrator supervises and manages the overall operation of the FoodRescue system.

Administrator responsibilities and functions include:

- Managing user accounts
- Approving or rejecting recipient registrations
- Managing donor and volunteer accounts
- Monitoring food listings and requests
- Removing invalid listings
- Resolving issues and disputes
- Managing food categories and system data
- Monitoring successful and failed redistributions
- Viewing system statistics and reports
- Managing notifications and relevant system settings


## 1.7 Major Features

FoodRescue will provide the following major system features:

### 1.7.1 Authentication and User Management

The system will provide registration, login, logout, password management, profile management, account status management, and role-based access control for different categories of users.

### 1.7.2 Food Surplus Listing Management

Donors will be able to create and manage surplus-food listings by providing information such as food name, category, quantity, preparation time, listing time, expiry or use-by time, dietary type, packaging information, pickup location, description, and special handling requirements.

### 1.7.3 Food Availability and Status Tracking

The system will track the lifecycle of a food listing through statuses such as Draft, Available, Requested, Allocated, Pickup Pending, Collected, Delivered, Expired, and Cancelled.

### 1.7.4 Food Search and Filtering

Eligible recipients will be able to browse and search available food listings and filter them using criteria such as food category, dietary type, quantity, location, expiry urgency, availability, and listing date.

### 1.7.5 Rule-Based Food Prioritization

The system will calculate the priority of available food listings using predefined rules based on factors such as expiry urgency, quantity, distance, recipient suitability, and listing age. Listings may be classified as High, Medium, or Low Priority.

### 1.7.6 Recipient Matching

The system will identify potentially suitable recipients using criteria such as food category, dietary compatibility, required quantity, recipient capacity, geographic proximity, availability, eligibility, and expiry urgency.

### 1.7.7 Request and Allocation Management

Recipients will be able to request available food. Authorized users will be able to review requests and approve or reject them. Approved requests will result in an allocation that begins the pickup and delivery process.

### 1.7.8 Volunteer Pickup and Delivery Coordination

The system will support volunteer assignments for approved allocations and allow volunteers to manage pickup and delivery activities through status updates such as Assigned, Pickup Scheduled, Picked Up, In Transit, Delivered, Failed, and Cancelled.

### 1.7.9 Food Expiry Management

The system will monitor active food listings and identify expired food. Expired listings will be marked unavailable, new requests will be prevented, and affected users may be notified.

### 1.7.10 Notifications

The system will provide in-app notifications for important events, including new requests, approvals or rejections, volunteer assignments, pickup completion, delivery completion, cancellations, and approaching food expiry.

### 1.7.11 Role-Specific Dashboards

Different users will receive dashboards relevant to their roles.

- Donors will be able to view active listings, pending requests, completed donations, expired listings, and redistributed quantities.
- Recipients will be able to view available food, pending requests, approved requests, received food, and request history.
- Volunteers will be able to view assigned pickups, pending tasks, completed deliveries, and task history.
- Administrators will be able to monitor users, listings, requests, redistributions, expired food, deliveries, and system statistics.

### 1.7.12 Impact Tracking

The system will maintain measurable statistics such as total food listed, total food redistributed, total food expired, successful donations, recipient organizations served, completed pickups, and completed deliveries.

### 1.7.13 Administrative Management

Administrators will be able to manage users, verify recipients, monitor listings and requests, remove invalid listings, resolve issues, manage categories, view reports, and monitor overall system activity.


## 1.8 Assumptions

The following assumptions are made while defining and designing the FoodRescue system:

1. Users are expected to have access to an internet-connected device and a web browser to use the system.

2. Donors are assumed to provide accurate information about the surplus food, including quantity, preparation time, listing time, expiry or use-by time, and pickup location.

3. Recipients are assumed to provide valid profile and organizational information during registration.

4. Recipient accounts that require verification are assumed to be reviewed and approved by an administrator before they can request food.

5. Volunteers are assumed to provide accurate availability and service-area information for assignment purposes.

6. The pickup and delivery process is assumed to take place outside the software system, while FoodRescue records and tracks the relevant statuses and confirmations.

7. The system is assumed to use predefined business rules for food prioritization and recipient matching rather than Machine Learning or Artificial Intelligence.

8. Users are assumed to perform actions according to their assigned roles and to provide valid information when interacting with the system.

9. The database is assumed to be available to the application during normal system operation.

10. Notifications in the initial version are assumed to be delivered through the application's in-app notification mechanism.

11. The system is intended to operate within a defined service area for the initial implementation, with expansion to additional geographic areas considered as future scope.


## 1.9 Constraints

The development and operation of the FoodRescue system are subject to the following constraints:

1. The project is being developed as a college-level Software Engineering project, so the scope must remain realistic and achievable within the available academic timeline and resources.

2. The initial implementation will be a web-based system rather than a dedicated mobile application.

3. The core system will use rule-based prioritization and matching. Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence will not be required for the primary functionality.

4. The system will depend on a relational database for storing users, food listings, requests, allocations, notifications, and transaction records.

5. Real-world pickup and delivery activities are outside the direct control of the software. The system can record and track these activities but cannot guarantee successful physical collection or delivery.

6. The initial notification mechanism will primarily use in-app notifications. External SMS, WhatsApp, or similar notification services are outside the initial implementation scope.

7. Advanced GPS tracking and route optimization will not be part of the initial system.

8. The accuracy of information such as food quantity, expiry time, location, and availability depends on the information provided by users.

9. The initial implementation may operate within a limited geographic service area rather than supporting multi-city deployment.

10. The system's performance and availability will depend on the local development environment, database availability, and network connectivity.

11. The first version will prioritize core functionality and software engineering quality over implementing every possible future enhancement.
